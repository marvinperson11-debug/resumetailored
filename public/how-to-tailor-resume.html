How to Tailor Your Resume to Any Job Description

By the ResumeTailored team · Last updated November 4, 2026 · 8 min read
← How ATS resume screening works

A resume tailor rewrites your resume, truthfully, so its wording lines up with a specific job posting — not building a new resume from scratch, and never adding experience you don't have. The process below is the same one behind every honest tailoring job: find the posting's own language, confirm you genuinely have the matching experience, and rewrite your existing bullets using that language where it's accurate.

The step-by-step process

1. Get the full job posting, not just the title

Copy the entire description — responsibilities, required qualifications, and preferred qualifications. The required/preferred split matters: required items are usually what a screening filter checks first.

2. Pull out the posting's priority keywords

List the specific skills, tools, certifications, and job titles the posting repeats or lists under "requirements." These are the terms most likely to be checked against your resume — see how ATS screening works for why exact wording matters.

3. Match each keyword to real experience — or don't use it

For every keyword on your list, ask whether you've actually done that work. If yes, note which job and which result. If no, leave it out. This step is the one that keeps tailoring honest instead of just keyword-stuffing.

4. Rewrite your existing bullets in the posting's language

Where you have a genuine match, rewrite the bullet using the posting's own term instead of your own internal title for the same thing (e.g., if you did what the posting calls "account management" but you called it "client relationship work," use the posting's phrase — only if it's an accurate description of what you did).

5. Reorder for relevance

Put your most relevant experience and skills nearer the top of each section. An ATS and a human reviewer both tend to weight what comes first.

6. Check the formatting is ATS-readable

Single column, standard section headings, no tables or text boxes, a common font. Formatting that looks fine on screen can still parse badly — see the parsing section of how ATS screening works.

7. Check your match before you send it

Run the tailored resume and the job posting through an ATS checker to see which required terms are still missing, then decide — truthfully — whether you can address any of them.

A before/after example

This is a fictional, illustrative example — not a real candidate, employer, or generated output. The point is the method: the "after" bullet reuses only what the "before" bullet already says, rewritten in the posting's own terms.

Illustrative example Job posting (excerpt)
"We're looking for a Customer Success Manager who can manage a portfolio of client accounts, reduce churn, and lead quarterly business reviews using CRM data."
Before
"Managed client relationships and helped improve retention for an assigned portfolio of accounts. Used CRM software to track renewals and presented updates to clients each quarter."
After
"Customer Success Manager overseeing a portfolio of client accounts; used CRM data to track renewals and reduce churn, and led quarterly business reviews with clients."

Every fact in "After" — the portfolio of accounts, the CRM tool use, the quarterly client presentations, the retention/churn outcome — already appears in "Before." Nothing is added: "churn" and "quarterly business reviews" are just the posting's own words for retention and the quarterly updates the candidate already did. No new employer, number, or skill was introduced.

Checklist

  • Copied the full job posting, not just the title
  • Listed the specific skills, tools, and titles the posting repeats
  • Matched each keyword to real experience — and left out the ones that don't apply
  • Rewrote existing bullets in the posting's own language, where accurate
  • Reordered sections so the most relevant experience comes first
  • Checked formatting is single-column and ATS-readable
  • Ran it through an ATS checker and reviewed what's still missing
  • Proofread for accuracy — nothing added that isn't true
