Resume Writing for Marketing Managers: A Step-by-Step Guide

Marketing hiring has moved from activity to accountability. A marketing manager’s resume should tie your campaigns to business results, show the budget and team you ran, and name the channels and tools the posting asks for.

What hiring teams screen for

  • Business impact: pipeline, revenue influenced, leads, customer acquisition cost, conversion and retention, not only impressions or likes.
  • Scope: budget managed, team and agency size, channels owned and markets served.
  • The marketing mix you own: demand generation, content, product marketing, lifecycle or brand.
  • Technical comfort: analytics, marketing automation and CRM tools, and testing discipline.
  • Management skills: planning, prioritization, stakeholder alignment with sales and product, and hiring.

Certifications and credentials

  • No license required. Marketing has no mandatory license, so hiring teams decide on results, portfolio and interviews. Certifications are optional proof of tool skills.
  • Platform certifications. Google Analytics, Google Ads, Meta Blueprint, HubSpot and Salesforce Marketing Cloud certifications show tool skill. List the name, the issuer and the year, and only those relevant to the role.
  • Professional marketing credentials. Credentials such as a marketing degree or an MBA can help for senior roles. Add the school and year in Education.
  • Portfolio and links. A link to campaigns, a case study or a writing portfolio, when you can share it, turns claims into evidence. Add it to the header.

Section by section: building the resume

1. Header and links

Name, contact details, city, LinkedIn and a portfolio or case-study link. Marketing recruiters judge your personal presentation too, so keep the header clean and consistent with your LinkedIn.

2. Summary: scope, channels, result

Two to three lines giving your level, the type of marketing you lead, the channels, and one headline result.

B2B marketing manager with [X] years leading demand generation for a $[X]M SaaS company. Owned a $[X]K budget and a team of [X]; grew marketing-sourced pipeline by [X]% in [X] months.Illustrative only. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own real numbers; never invent them.

3. Experience: campaign, action, business result

Write each bullet as a short case: the goal, what you did, and the business result. Replace vanity metrics with outcomes: leads, pipeline, revenue, cost per acquisition, conversion, retention. Include the budget and team size at the top of each role. If you do not have revenue data, use the closest measured result and say what it influenced.

Rebuilt the nurture program across [X] segments in HubSpot, lifting MQL-to-SQL conversion from [X]% to [X]% and reducing cost per qualified lead by [X]%.Illustrative only. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own real numbers; never invent them.

4. Show testing and learning

Add one bullet on experiments, such as A/B tests, channel reallocation or killing a failing campaign. It shows judgment and a data-driven approach.

5. Show leadership and cross-functional work

Marketing managers work with sales, product and finance. Add a bullet on alignment, such as shared pipeline targets with sales, launch plans with product, or agency management, with an outcome.

6. Skills and tools

List channels, analytics, automation and CRM tools by name in short groups. Match the posting: if it says “GA4”, include “Google Analytics 4 (GA4)”.

7. Education and format

List your degree, relevant certifications, and any publications or speaking. Keep to one page up to about ten years of experience and two for senior leaders, in a single-column layout.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reporting activity (“managed social media”) rather than impact on leads, pipeline or revenue.
  • Leaving out the budget and team size, which show your level.
  • Using vanity metrics such as followers and impressions without business context.
  • Listing every tool. Name the ones that match the posting and that you can discuss in detail.
  • Overstating credit for results that sales or product drove. State your contribution precisely, such as “sourced” versus “influenced”.
  • Skipping a portfolio when you are allowed to share one.

Where to apply, and what the ATS will do

Marketing roles are posted on LinkedIn, general boards and company career pages, which commonly use applicant tracking systems such as Greenhouse, Lever and Workday. Recruiters search on channel, tool and industry terms, then check your LinkedIn profile for consistency.

  • LinkedIn for roles, recruiter outreach and networking; make your headline name your specialty.
  • Company career pages and general boards such as Indeed.
  • Agencies and specialist recruiters for brand, performance and in-house marketing roles.
  • Mirror the posting’s language: “demand generation” is not always “lead generation” to a screening system.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What metrics should a marketing manager resume include?⌄
Pipeline or revenue contribution, leads and conversion rates, customer acquisition cost, return on ad spend, retention, and the budget and team you managed. Show the time frame and your own contribution.
How do I show results if I do not have revenue numbers?⌄
Use the closest measured outcomes, such as qualified leads, conversion lift or cost per lead, and say what they were for. If you cannot share numbers, use a percentage change.
Should I include a portfolio?⌄
Yes, if you can share work. Link two or three case studies with your role, the goal and the measured result.
Do I need Google Analytics or HubSpot certifications?⌄
They are optional. They help if you are changing roles or the posting names the tool, and they are less important than showing how you used it.
How long should a marketing manager resume be?⌄
One page for early-career managers and up to two pages for senior marketers with several relevant roles.
