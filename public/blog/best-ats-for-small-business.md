## What a small business actually needs from an ATS

Small teams don't need every feature; they need the ones that stop good candidates from slipping through. Seven things matter most:

- **Setup measured in minutes.** If it needs an implementation project, you'll hire the old way before it's ready.
- **One-form job posting.** Title, description, requirements, location, salary, type and deadline, with no workflow builder in the way.
- **A visible pipeline.** Every applicant has a status you can see at a glance, from new to hired.
- **Interviews and messages in the same place.** Scheduling and candidate conversations shouldn't live in three different inboxes.
- **Basic reporting.** A hiring funnel and time-to-hire, with an export when someone asks for the numbers.
- **A fast path for urgent roles.** Hourly and same-day work shouldn't need the same process as a senior hire.
- **A price that starts at zero and scales.** You shouldn't need a contract before you've made a single hire.

## Your three realistic options

For a small business, the choice is almost always among three approaches:

| Approach | Cost | Setup | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet + shared inbox | Free | Immediate | Applicants scatter across email, nobody knows who replied to whom, and there's no pipeline or history. It gets worse with every extra reviewer. |
| Enterprise ATS | $$$ | An implementation project | Powerful, but built for large talent-acquisition teams. Small teams pay for and configure features they'll never use. |
| Lightweight ATS (the Employer Portal) | Free to start; $49, $99 or $299 per month | Minutes, self-serve | Designed for small teams. If you run high-volume hiring across many departments, an enterprise system is likely a better fit. |

> Rule of thumb: if you can't describe your hiring process on one page, you may need an enterprise ATS. If you can, you almost certainly don't.

## When it's not the right fit

Being honest about this is the point of a buyer's guide. The Employer Portal is built for small teams. If you're running a large, multi-department hiring operation with a dedicated recruiting team and custom approval workflows, an enterprise ATS is built for that and a lightweight tool isn't trying to be one. And if you hire once or twice a year with only a handful of applicants, a spreadsheet may be all you need.

- Only pay for the tier you can name a reason for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best ATS for a small business?
The best small business ATS is the one your team will actually use: quick to set up, a clear pipeline from new applicant to hired, interview scheduling and messaging in the same place, basic hiring analytics, and a price that doesn't assume a dedicated recruiting department. ### Do I really need an applicant tracking system, or is a spreadsheet enough?
A spreadsheet works for one or two roles with a handful of applicants. It starts to fail when applications arrive by email, several people review candidates, or you need to know who has been contacted. An ATS gives every applicant one record, one status and one message thread, which is what stops candidates from being lost or ghosted.
