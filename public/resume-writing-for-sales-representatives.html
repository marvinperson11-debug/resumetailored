Resume Writing for Sales Representatives: A Step-by-Step Guide

A sales resume is judged like a sales call: it should lead with results. Hiring managers want your quota attainment, your deal sizes and your selling motion, and the ATS wants the CRM and sales terms the posting names.

What hiring teams screen for

  • Quota attainment, with the period and the quota size or deal range, ideally ranked against your team.
  • The kind of selling you did: inbound or outbound, SMB, mid-market or enterprise, field or inside, new business or account management.
  • Deal metrics: average contract value, sales-cycle length, pipeline you generated and your win rate.
  • The tools you work in: CRM, sales-engagement and prospecting software.
  • Industry and product knowledge: software, medical devices, insurance, staffing, or industrial supplies all sell differently.

Licenses and certifications: when they apply

  • Most sales roles need no license. Software, business-services and most B2B roles hire on results. Spend your space on numbers and deals instead of credentials.
  • Regulated products. Insurance sales requires a state producer license, financial products may require FINRA securities registrations (such as Series 7 and 63), and real-estate sales require a state license. List the license, the state or registration, and the status, near the top for these roles.
  • Sales methodology training. Training in MEDDIC, Challenger, SPIN or Sandler can be listed in one line if you actually use the method. It shows you sell with a process.
  • Tool certifications. Salesforce and HubSpot certifications are worth listing for roles that depend on them, such as sales operations or revenue-focused positions.

Section by section: building the resume

1. Header and a results headline

Name, contact details and LinkedIn. Under it, one headline line that names your sales type and your best number, because a recruiter reads that line first.

B2B SaaS Account Executive | [X]% of quota, [X] consecutive quarters | Mid-market, $[X]K average deal sizeIllustrative only. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own real numbers; never invent them.

2. Summary: segment, motion, proof

Two to three lines. State who you sell to, how you sell and one proof point. Avoid “results-driven”; the numbers do the work.

3. Experience: the number first

Each bullet should lead with the result, then say how. Use quota attainment, revenue closed, pipeline built, deal size, new logos, and retention. State the time frame so the number is believable. Add rankings (for example “top 3 of 12 reps”) when you have them, and say whether the quota was individual or team.

Closed $[X] in new ARR against a $[X] quota ([X]% attainment), ranking #[X] of [X] reps, by building a [X]-account outbound list and running [X] discovery calls a week.Illustrative only. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own real numbers; never invent them.

4. Show the process, not only the outcome

Add one or two bullets on how you work: how you qualify, how you handle multi-threaded deals, how you forecast. Managers hire process because it repeats.

5. Skills and tools

A short line each for CRM, prospecting and engagement tools, and sales methodology. List the exact product names. If the posting says “Salesforce”, write “Salesforce” and not only “CRM”.

6. Awards, education and extras

Add club or award recognition with the year, such as “President’s Club 2024”. Education goes at the bottom unless the role asks for a degree. A short Languages line helps for territory-based roles.

7. Format and length

One page for most sales roles; two for senior or multi-company enterprise histories. Use a single-column layout, bold the key metrics sparingly, and keep dates consistent so the parser reads tenure correctly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reporting activity instead of results: “made 100 calls a day” is a weaker statement than the pipeline it produced.
  • Leaving out the quota or the time period, which makes a percentage meaningless.
  • Inflating numbers. Reference checks and CRM records are easy to verify; use real figures or ranges.
  • Listing only “CRM” and no product name, which a keyword search will not match.
  • Hiding gaps and short stints; add a short context line if a startup folded or a territory was cut.
  • Using the same resume for a transactional inside-sales role and a long-cycle enterprise role. The emphasis should change.

Where to apply, and what the ATS will do

Many sales teams recruit on LinkedIn and through applicant tracking systems such as Greenhouse, Lever and Workday, and recruiters often search by title, tool and industry. Because sales recruiters also call candidates quickly, keep your phone number and location current.

  • LinkedIn: keep your profile numbers consistent with the resume, because managers will check both.
  • Company career pages and general boards such as Indeed for volume.
  • Specialist recruiters and staffing firms for software, medical-device and industrial sales.
  • Referrals from former managers are the highest-value route: send a short note with your attainment history.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do I show quota attainment on a resume?⌄
State the percentage, the period and the size of the quota or the revenue closed, for example “112% of a $900K annual quota”. Add your rank among peers when you have it.
What if I missed quota or was in a down market?⌄
Show what you controlled: pipeline you built, activity-to-meeting conversion, deals saved, and how you improved over time. Do not list a missed number without context; rank and improvement can help.
Do I need a sales license?⌄
Only for regulated products, such as insurance, securities and real estate. Check your state and the product’s rules, and list the license and status near the top.
I am new to sales. What goes on my resume?⌄
Use transferable results: customer-facing work, upsells, fundraising, campus or club recruiting. Add any prospecting or CRM exposure, plus a short sales course or certification if you have one.
How long should a sales resume be?⌄
One page for most reps, two pages for senior sellers with several companies. Lead each role with your strongest number.
